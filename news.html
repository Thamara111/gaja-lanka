Kawanthissa: The Fallen Galgamuwa Wild Giant Whose Death Shook Sri Lanka

Regarded by wildlife biologists and photographers as the tallest and most magnificent wild tusker in Sri Lanka, standing nearly 11 feet tall. In late September 2026, Kawanthissa was found crippled by a severe poacher gunshot injury to his right foreleg in the Galgamuwa region. Department of Wildlife Conservation veterinarians treated him daily for over 3 weeks. Despite intensive care, he stopped eating and tragically collapsed in a Siyambalangamuwa paddy field, passing away on October 16, 2026 at 6:18 PM. Following public grief, a suspect was arrested with firearms and an independent ministerial inquiry was ordered.

Agbo of Thirappane: The Nation's United Battle to Save a Wounded Wild Tusker

Sri Lanka's most closely monitored living wild tusker. In June 2023, Agbo was discovered near Thirappane crippled by a poacher gunshot blast to his left front leg. An unprecedented national rescue initiative mobilized DWC veterinary surgeons, military engineers, and local farmers. Over 18 months of continuous antibiotic darting, specialized harness slings, and GPS collar telemetry, Agbo survived and continues to roam the dry-zone cascade as a living symbol of resilience and human compassion.

Mahasen of Minneriya: The Sovereign Bull of the Great Asian Gathering

Named after ancient King Mahasen who engineered the 4,670-acre Minneriya reservoir in the 3rd century, this magnificent wild tusker is the undisputed sovereign of the annual Minneriya Elephant Gathering. Possessing some of the longest and most symmetrical ivory tusks among wild Asian elephants, Mahasen commands deep respect among the wild matriarchal herds and draws wildlife researchers and naturalists from around the world.

Galgamuwa Dala Poottuwa: The Wild Crossed-Tusk King Whose Murder Awakened a Nation

A mythic wild tusker celebrated for his extraordinarily rare 'Dala Poottuwa' morphology—tusks that crossed over each other symmetrically in front of his trunk. Partially blind from shotgun pellet traps, he roamed the tank cascades peacefully until November 2017, when ivory poachers gunned him down. His death triggered massive nationwide protests, special task force anti-poaching operations, and strict penal revisions in Parliament protecting wild tuskers.

Nadungamuwa Raja: The Gentle Titan Who Walked 90km for Sacred Duty

Revered as the tallest tamed tusker in Asia (10.5 ft). Gifted by a Mysore prince in 1953, he served as the casket bearer of the Sacred Tooth Relic for 15 consecutive seasons. He refused vehicular transport and walked 90 km from Gampaha to Kandy each August under armed military escorts. Declared a National Cultural Treasure by Presidential decree, he was posthumously honored with a state stamp and museum preservation.

Maligawa Raja: The 50-Year Sovereign of the Sacred Tooth Relic

Captured in Batticaloa in 1925 and gifted to the Temple of the Tooth in 1937, Raja carried the golden casket for 50 years without an incident. His poise amidst booming cannons and drumming became legend. The President proclaimed a national day of mourning upon his death in 1988, and his taxidermied remains are enshrined in Kandy's dedicated Raja Museum.

Millangoda Raja: The Kegalle Giant with Asia's Longest Tusks

Captured in Nawagaththegama in 1945, Millangoda Raja bore the longest recorded tusks of any living Asian elephant in modern history—measuring 7.5 feet (2.3 meters) each. He carried the Sacred Tooth Relic for decades and led the Kelaniya Duruthu Perahera. He was buried with Buddhist rites and military honors in Kegalle in 2011.
