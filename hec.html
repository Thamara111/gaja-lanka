Taxonomy of Interaction • Root Cause Analysis

Deconstructing the Landscape Conflict

Contrary to common belief, Sri Lankan elephants cannot simply be confined within Department of Wildlife Conservation (DWC) protected areas. Science demonstrates that parks cover only 30% of their actual biological home ranges.

warning Key Ecological Insight

Over 70% of wild elephant range overlaps directly with human agricultural lands.

Elephants need massive, contiguous landscapes to secure water and forage across dry and wet seasons. When linear infrastructure, settlements, and continuous monoculture slice through home ranges, interactions become inevitable. Solutions must occur on shared private and communal grounds—not purely behind national park boundaries.

Vector A • Corridor Loss

Agricultural Encroachment

Unplanned conversion of scrublands and traditional dry-zone swidden (chena) tracts into permanent fenced properties blocks multi-generational paths connecting dry-season reservoirs (tanks).

Result: Herd displacement into residential zones
energy_savings_leaf
Vector B • Caloric Incentive

High-Calorie Cash Crops

Cultivated crops—paddy rice, sweet bananas, sugarcane, and hybrid maize—contain up to five times the nutritional density of wild grass. Elephants achieve maximum caloric replenishment in minimal grazing time.

Result: Habituation of adult solitary bulls
fence
Vector C • Spatial Fragmentation

Uncoordinated Linear Fencing

Misplaced permanent electric fences erected along arbitrary administrative boundaries isolate herds on small pockets of scrubland, forcing them through village settlement clusters to reach essential water points.

Result: Fence sabotage and escalated nighttime confrontations
Empirical Engineering • Science of Fencing

Community-Managed Seasonal Electric Fences

Pioneered by the Centre for Conservation and Research (CCR) in partnership with rural agrarian societies, seasonal fencing represents a paradigm shift from rigid territorial exclusion to dynamic, time-bound harvest protection.

Conventional Model: Failing Strategy cancel

Permanent Forest Boundary Fences

Erected on jungle perimeters kilometers away from villages. Villagers have no sense of ownership, so solar energizers and wire strands fall into disrepair. Inside the forest, during the dry season, grass dries up and elephants are starving—giving them extreme motivation to break through fence lines with logs or tusks.

close Elephants trapped on cultivated lands unable to return.
close High annual state maintenance costs with frequent failures.
close Zero agrarian community involvement in fence patrols.
CCR Benchmark: Validated Coexistence check_circle

Temporary Seasonal Paddy Enclosures

Fences are deployed only around the collective village paddy tract during the 3-4 months of crop maturation. When the rice is harvested, the community packs away the wire and solar energizers. Elephants are then free to graze on the remaining nutrient-rich stubble during fallow months without food motivation to damage equipment.

done Farmers construct and patrol the fence themselves every single night.
done Post-harvest landscape remains open for natural seasonal elephant migration.
done Eliminates the cycle of hostility and lethal farmer retaliation.

Four-Stage Community Lifecycle Architecture

01
Agrarian Pre-Sow Assembly

Village farmers convene at the local tank temple to form a patrol roster and verify high-tensile wire reels and solar battery arrays.

02
Cultivation Grid Activation

Posts are driven around the perimeter of the joint cultivation zone. 6,000–8,000V non-lethal pulsed energizers run from 18:00 to 06:00 daily.

03
Bimodal Daily Circuit Check

Assigned community teams test ground voltage morning and dusk, clearing encroaching grass weeds that could ground the electrical charge.

04
Harvest Deconstruction

Within 72 hours of paddy harvesting, wires and components are stored securely. Elephants enter freely to feed on stubble safely.

Infrasonic Array Network

Elephants communicate using rumbles below 20Hz that travel several kilometers through seismic and acoustic waves. Sub-audible ground and microphone nodes pinpoint moving herds 45 minutes before they reach farm gates.

Elephants avoid the painful stings of bees around sensitive ears and trunks, retreating immediately upon hearing hive frequencies. Interplanting with spiny lime (Citrus aurantiifolia) creates impenetrable, lucrative cash crop borders.

Human Security & Field Protocols

Farmer & Villager Non-Lethal Safety Field Code

Proven behavioral guidelines developed by field biologists and village elders to safeguard human lives during unexpected close-quarter night encounters.

flashlight_on Illumination Protocol

Focused Ground Beams, Not Faces

Never shine ultra-bright LED spotlights directly into an elephant's eyes. Blinding an adult elephant causes acute panic, disorients them, and frequently triggers defensive frontal charges. Cast high-lumen light downward onto the ground in front of the animal to signal human presence without blinding.

• Prohibited: Strobes flashed directly into eyes
volume_off Acoustic Restraint

Replace Explosives with Controlled Acoustics

Cease using unmanaged firecrackers (Ali Vediya) inside village lanes. Indiscriminate loud explosions wound animals, deafen them, and condition bulls into aggressive combat response. Use unified metallic acoustic clanging from safe watchtowers or vehicular engine revving from designated shelters.

• Prohibited: Improvised explosives and traps
cottage Shelter Architecture

Elevated Watchtowers (Pela)

Never sleep on ground-level verandas during the harvest season. Agrarian watch-houses (Pela) must be built at least 5 meters off the ground on thick, sturdy mature trees or reinforced concrete pillared platforms with high clearance and quick escape ladder cords.

• Minimum elevation: 5.2 meters from ground
directions_walk Escape Tactics

Downwind Retreat & Lateral Movement

Elephants have poor eyesight but an extraordinary sense of smell. If surprised at close quarters on foot, never run in a straight line down open roads. Retreat downwind, move at a sharp diagonal angle, and drop a backpack or cloth to catch their olfactory investigation and buy escape time.

• Critical: Never corner a mother and calf
kitchen Domestic Storage

Aroma-Sealed Grain Granaries

Domestic house break-ins occur because elephants smell bagged unhusked rice or jaggery through clay brick walls. Grain stores (Bissa) should be constructed freestanding 30 meters away from family sleeping quarters with airtight lime sealing to prevent vapor trails.

• Structural buffer: 30m away from bedroom
