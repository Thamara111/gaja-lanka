Myths vs. Ecological Realities

cancel Myth: Park Fencing Works

Believing that wild elephants can be permanently penned inside national park boundaries using perimeter electric fences.

Ecological Reality: 70% of wild elephant range in Sri Lanka falls outside protected parks. Fences must protect villages and cultivation, while leaving natural corridors open.
cancel Myth: All Bulls Have Tusks

Assuming that male Sri Lankan elephants universally grow elongated ivory tusks like African elephants or Indian counterparts.

Ecological Reality: Less than 2.5% of Sri Lankan bulls carry tusks (Ath-Dhal). Over 97% are naturally tuskless tush-bearing bulls (Aliya), an evolutionary hallmark.
cancel Myth: Captivity Equals Safety

Believing that captive shelter and tethering offer a humane alternative to the harsh dry-zone wild environment.

Ecological Reality: Wild herds roam 10–20 km daily with complex matrilineal social bonds. Captive tethering causes chronic stereotypic pacing, foot rot, and social trauma.
