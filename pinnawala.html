PAGE 01 · PINNAWALA ELEPHANT ORPHANAGE

A LIVING SYMBOL OF
ELEPHANT CONSERVATION IN SRI LANKA

Welcome to Pinnawala Elephant Orphanage, one of Sri Lanka's best-known elephant-care institutions.

Established to care for orphaned and vulnerable elephants, Pinnawala has developed into a major center for long-term elephant management, breeding, education, and public engagement.

Visitors can learn about elephant behavior and daily care while observing the herd and its routines. The institution also offers an opportunity to reflect on the responsibilities involved in caring for elephants under human management.

01. THE LEGACY OF PINNAWALA

From five orphaned calves to a nationally recognized institution

Pinnawala Elephant Orphanage was established on 16 February 1975 by the Department of Wildlife Conservation. Its original herd consisted of five orphaned calves: Kumari, Neela, Kadira, Vijaya, and Mathali.

A major milestone followed in 1984, when Sukumali became the first elephant calf born at the institution. The orphanage subsequently developed a captive breeding programme, and the supplied institutional content records the birth of twin calves in 2021.

Pinnawala's history reflects the changing approaches to elephant care in Sri Lanka, including the management of orphaned elephants, veterinary care, breeding, public education, and animal welfare.

02. LIFE AT PINNAWALA

Understanding the daily rhythms of the herd

Life at Pinnawala follows a managed routine that includes movement between designated areas, feeding, river bathing, rest, and veterinary care.

Observing these routines helps visitors understand the physical and social needs of elephants and the responsibilities of caring for a large herd.

Ma Oya River Bath

The Ma Oya River Sessions

Twice daily, the elephants walk in procession down to the wide, rocky Ma Oya River. Hydrotherapy cools body temperature, cleanses thick skin, and provides vital herd play and socialization.

Visitor Recommendation

The 10:00 AM – 12:00 PM session offers the most extensive herd viewing across the river rapids from riverside verandas.

03. MEET THE HERD

Every elephant has a story

Behind the institution's public identity are individual elephants with different histories, personalities, and care needs.

Use this section to introduce selected elephants through verified profiles, archival photographs, and stories documented by the institution.

BORN 1984

Sukumali

First captive-born calf at Pinnawala

Background: Born in 1984, Sukumali's birth was a historic breakthrough for the facility, demonstrating the potential for managed captive breeding in Sri Lanka.

Care & Welfare: Ongoing veterinary geriatric monitoring, specialized dietary fiber, and prominent standing within the herd matriarchal structure.

Breeding Milestone Cohort
ARRIVED 1975

Kumari

Original Founding Calf

Background: One of the five original calves brought to Pinnawala when it opened on 16 February 1975 under the Department of Wildlife Conservation.

Care & Welfare: Lifelong specialized management, gentle matriarchal temperament, and daily participation in Ma Oya river baths.

Founding 1975 Nucleus
ARRIVED 1975

Neela & Kadira

Original Founding Herd

Background: Rescued in 1975 as vulnerable wild calves separated from maternal herds, forming the pioneering nucleus of Pinnawala.

Care & Welfare: Routine podiatric examinations, managed socialization in spacious paddocks, and specialized nutrient-rich browse.

Founding 1975 Nucleus
ARRIVED 1975

Vijaya & Mathali

Original Calves & Early Matriarchs

Background: Founding members of the 1975 rescue cohort. Mathali subsequently gave birth to Sukumali in 1984, establishing captive breeding lineage.

Care & Welfare: Senior elephant nutritional care, joint health supplements, and extensive mud wallow enrichment.

Founding 1975 Nucleus
BORN 2021 · HISTORIC TWINS

The 2021 Twin Calves

Historic Veterinary Milestone (Mother: Surangi)

Background: Pinnawala recorded the birth of twin elephant calves in August 2021, an extraordinarily rare biological event in Asian elephants and the first recorded twin birth at the facility in over 80 years.

Care & Welfare: Intensive supplemental milk nutrition, 24-hour veterinary monitoring, and specialized nursery paddock space to support twin nursing demands.

04. CONSERVATION, BREEDING & ANIMAL WELFARE

Protecting elephants through responsible care

Pinnawala's role includes long-term care, veterinary management, breeding, and public education. These activities also raise important questions about captive elephant welfare, social needs, movement, enrichment, and the quality of human care.

A responsible conservation experience should help visitors understand both the institution's history and the ongoing need for evidence-based elephant welfare.

medical_services

Veterinary Care & Health Monitoring

Full-time resident wildlife veterinarians conduct daily health checks, podiatric foot care, wound management, and geriatric care for elderly elephants.

nutrition

Elephant Nutrition & Daily Care

Each adult elephant consumes hundreds of kilograms of kitul palm, jak leaves, grass, and supplementary minerals daily, supplemented by clean river hydration.

groups

Social Relationships & Herd Behavior

Observing how calves interact with older cows, herd matriarch dynamics during river crossings, and social play during river bathing sessions.

family_history

Captive Breeding & Population Management

Documenting generational births from Sukumali in 1984 to the 2021 twins, analyzing captive reproductive viability and genetic diversity.

verified_user

Responsible Tourism & Animal Welfare

Balancing public educational access with animal welfare, ensuring strict adherence to non-invasive viewing distances, banning abusive equipment, and fostering public consciousness of wild elephant conservation.
